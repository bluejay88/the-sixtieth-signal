# Keep the Variable — Episode 01 Pilot Rundown

Status: internal draft; author approval required before recording or publication.

## Episode identity

- Working title: “The Variable That Should Not Exist”
- Gate: pre-COUNT
- Spoiler level: Chapters 1–4 only
- Target edited duration: 42–50 minutes
- Canon rule: quote or summarize only the publication-locked manuscript. Panel speculation must be labeled as interpretation.

## Panel roles

1. Host / investigative listener — controls pace and distinguishes fact from framing.
2. Systems analyst — tests what LOAM’s worsening forecast actually establishes.
3. Climate or polar-science reader — challenges physical assumptions without pretending to authenticate fictional evidence.
4. Historian of science — examines how institutions classify anomalies.
5. Ethics and governance reader — asks who may investigate, disclose, or withhold.
6. Literary critic — tracks narrative form, metaphor, and reader expectation.

These are editorial roles, not claims that real experts have been booked.

## Rundown

### 00:00–01:15 — Cold open

- Use the approved promise: “LOAM was built to predict famine. It finds Antarctica instead.”
- Three seconds of clean silence.
- Host: “What does a model owe us when removing the impossible answer makes every ordinary answer worse?”
- Music: original six-note signal cell only; licensing must be documented.

### 01:15–04:00 — Spoiler boundary and evidence rule

- State that discussion covers Chapters 1–4.
- Explain the panel rule: every claim receives a challenge and a steelman.
- Ask: “What does the evidence not entitle us to say?”

### 04:00–10:00 — What LOAM actually detects

- Establish the model’s logistics and famine-prediction purpose.
- Separate correlation, predictive usefulness, mechanism, and authorship.
- Pause point: five seconds after the first clear distinction between “real signal” and “known meaning.”

### 10:00–17:00 — Cassian and the temptation of control

- Discuss Cassian as builder, skeptic, and responsible party.
- Challenge: does technical fluency make him more reliable or merely more persuasive?
- Steelman his resistance to pattern hunger.

### 17:00–24:00 — Nico’s public-interest argument

- Contrast broadcast instinct with responsible listening.
- Ask what makes disclosure journalism rather than spectacle.
- Do not imply later plot outcomes.

### 24:00–31:00 — The institutional problem

- Discuss how organizations classify anomalous evidence.
- Separate secrecy needed for validation from secrecy used as command.
- Pause point: “When does caution become ownership of everyone else’s risk?”

### 31:00–38:00 — Listener roundtable

- Question 1: What result would make you distrust the model?
- Question 2: Who should be allowed to inspect the anomaly?
- Question 3: What evidence would justify travel to Antarctica?
- Invite responses only after community and moderation channels are approved.

### 38:00–44:00 — Competing interpretations

- Each panel role offers one hypothesis and one falsifier.
- Host labels all hypotheses as non-canonical interpretation.
- No ranking by drama; rank by testability.

### 44:00–end — Close

- Restate the spoiler boundary.
- Preview the COUNT discussion without revealing it.
- CTA options, subject to approval: continue reading, request the free edition, or join LOAM Archives.
- Leave two seconds of clean silence after the last spoken line.
