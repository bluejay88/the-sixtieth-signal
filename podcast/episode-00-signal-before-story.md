# Episode 0 — Signal Before Story

Status: approval-ready draft, not recorded or published  
Series: *Unfinished Evidence: The Sixtieth Signal Companion*  
Spoiler boundary: premise and jacket-level positioning only  
Target duration: 18–22 minutes  

## Panel identities

These are editorial perspectives, not fictional book characters and not claims that autonomous agents have independently read or endorsed the novel.

1. Host — welcoming, curious, controls spoilers and pace.
2. Science editor — tests the hard-science promise without declaring the story's theories factual.
3. Systems analyst — examines prediction, uncertainty, and model governance.
4. Literary critic — tracks form, ambiguity, and emotional stakes.
5. Audio director — explains voice and sound choices without revealing plot.
6. Ethics researcher — asks who has authority to interpret evidence.
7. Community reader — represents reader expectations and accessibility.
8. Skeptical producer — challenges hype, unsupported claims, and unclear calls to action.

## Recording rules

- Do not disclose events beyond the public premise.
- Do not present fictional science, institutions, archives, or signals as real.
- Do not claim the audiobook is complete; say it is in production.
- Do not improvise canon. Mark uncertain facts for author review.
- Keep character voices out of the panel; this show must remain sonically distinct from the retail audiobook.
- Announce synthetic-voice use in credits if any panel voice is generated.

## Script

### Cold open — 0:00–0:35

**HOST:** LOAM was built to predict famine. It finds Antarctica instead.

[Two beats of silence. A restrained six-note signal motif enters, then falls under the voice.]

**HOST:** That is the doorway into *The Sixtieth Signal*. This is *Unfinished Evidence*, a companion conversation about the questions around the novel—without turning the questions into answers the story has not earned.

### Welcome and boundaries — 0:35–2:00

**HOST:** This opening episode contains no plot discussion beyond the public premise. Later episodes will sit at six optional reading gates: COUNT, COMPARE, TURN, OVERLAY, WITNESS, and RETURN. You can stop at a gate, listen, and return to the book without crossing the next spoiler boundary.

**SKEPTICAL PRODUCER:** And we should make the commercial boundary just as clear. This companion is commentary. It is not the novel, and it is not evidence that the fictional events are real.

**HOST:** Exactly. The audiobook is currently in production. When an episode, sample, or chapter becomes available, its page should say precisely what has been published—not what is merely planned.

### The promise — 2:00–5:00

**LITERARY CRITIC:** The premise begins with a reversal. A system searches for one kind of absence and encounters another. That is a strong thriller mechanism, but it also asks a literary question: what do we notice only because our original model fails?

**SYSTEMS ANALYST:** Prediction systems do not simply reveal the world. They rank signals according to objectives, assumptions, and available data. So the interesting phrase is not only “it finds Antarctica.” It is “was built to predict famine.” The purpose of the system shapes the surprise.

**SCIENCE EDITOR:** My standard for hard science fiction is not whether every dramatic event is already possible. It is whether the story respects evidence, uncertainty, scale, and consequence. The companion show should preserve that standard. We can ask how a model might detect an anomaly without pretending that the novel is a scientific paper.

**COMMUNITY READER:** For a new reader, that gives me a useful expectation: this is a mystery powered by systems and evidence, but the reason to keep reading is what those systems do to people.

### Three readings — 5:00–8:30

**HOST:** The second public hook says: “There are three stories in this book. Most readers will only find one.” What can we say about that without spoiling it?

**LITERARY CRITIC:** Only that the book invites more than one mode of attention. A reader may follow plot, pattern, or the architecture around the text. Those modes can coexist. None should be treated as a test of whether someone read the book correctly.

**ETHICS RESEARCHER:** That matters. Hidden layers can reward curiosity, but they can also create status hierarchies. The companion should never shame a reader for choosing the visible story.

**AUDIO DIRECTOR:** Audio adds another design problem. A visual pattern cannot be inserted into the retail master casually. If a signal layer receives sound, the author must approve a consistent treatment. Otherwise the canonical narration stays verbatim and the signal layer remains a clearly labeled bonus experience.

**SKEPTICAL PRODUCER:** Which is also why we should not advertise a “fully decoded audio edition” until one actually passes text, signal, and listening QA.

### What the voices must do — 8:30–12:00

**AUDIO DIRECTOR:** The cast needs contrast without caricature. Human identity comes from intention, rhythm, and emotional pressure—not exaggerated accents. LOAM must be intelligible and controlled. Alien voices, if the story requires them, must remain voices first; distortion should never bury language.

**COMMUNITY READER:** And accessibility is part of the art. A beautiful player still needs keyboard controls, transcripts, clear speaker labels, speed controls, and honest error messages.

**SYSTEMS ANALYST:** Consistency is essential. If one character's voice drifts between chapters, the listener loses a form of narrative memory. That is why every line assignment needs approval before synthesis.

**HOST:** So the production promise is not “many voices.” It is recognizable voices with stable identities, documented pronunciations, and enough restraint for the story to breathe.

### The questions to carry into Part I — 12:00–16:30

**HOST:** Let us give the listener three questions—no answers—to carry into the opening chapters.

**SCIENCE EDITOR:** First: when an anomaly appears, what would count as a serious attempt to disprove it?

**ETHICS RESEARCHER:** Second: who gets to decide when uncertain evidence becomes public knowledge?

**LITERARY CRITIC:** Third: when a system produces an unexpected result, are we learning about the world, the system, or the people who built it?

[One beat between each question. No dramatic sting.]

**SKEPTICAL PRODUCER:** I would add a listening instruction: notice when certainty comes from evidence and when it comes from someone's authority.

**COMMUNITY READER:** And notice what remains understandable even if you never pursue a hidden layer. The primary story has to stand on its own.

### Close — 16:30–18:30

**HOST:** Begin with Chapter One, “The Hunger Map.” The first optional companion stop arrives at Gate COUNT, after Chapter Eight. The website will mark that discussion clearly as spoilers through Chapter Eight.

**HOST:** You can join the newsletter for publication updates or the LOAM Archives for separately labeled world material and redacted-file experiences. Subscription terms, frequency, price, and cancellation must be shown before anyone joins.

**HOST:** *The Sixtieth Signal* is written by Jayla Harris. This companion episode is an editorial production draft and is not yet published. Until the next gate: count what the model cannot explain.

[Six-note motif. Two seconds of clean silence.]
