Later, Imani found her alone beside a vending machine that had stopped accepting network payment.

“You were right about the handoff risk,” Imani said.

Pike looked at her. “That is not the part they will remember.”

“Should it be?”

Pike fed a paper bill into the machine. It rejected the corner.

“I spent thirty years being useful because I could act while other people were still defining the problem. Now the definition of good procedure includes waiting while someone else acts badly.”

“That sounds painful.”

“It is inefficient.”

Imani almost smiled. “Pain has better public relations.”

Pike flattened the bill against the machine with her palm. “You think this proves the protocol works.”

“No. I think it proves the protocol produced a cost we can see.”

“And if the cost had been a death?”

“Then we would have had to look at that too.”

There was no satisfying answer. That was the point of the architecture, though neither woman said it.

At Gate 300, the archive produced the line Seren had preserved across three contradictory copies:

No single mind may certify the righteousness of its own irreversible action.

No one applauded. Cassian read it twice, then looked through the glass at the quorum correcting its own correction.
