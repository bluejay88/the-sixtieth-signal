If the archive contained redundant relationships that allowed damaged information to be detected—and, within limits, corrected—then the structure had not merely been patterned.

It had been designed to survive imperfect transmission. Cassian recognized the philosophy instantly.

LOAM’s logistics models used the same principle everywhere.

Never trust one warehouse count. Never trust one weather feed. Never trust one shipping manifest. Never trust one satellite.

Agreement among independent sources was information. Disagreement was also information.

Imani stared at the recovered carrier.

“Ancient scribes did this socially.”

Cassian looked at her.

“Checksums?”

“Not like this. But copying traditions develop correction mechanisms. Repetition. Formulae. Parallel manuscripts. Teacher exemplars. Colophons. Scribal conventions.”

“Human error correction.”

“Yes.”

She looked almost offended by how universal the problem was.

“Preserving knowledge is always a fight against noise.”

LOAM generated six candidate reconstructions of the missing archive segment.

Only one satisfied all previously registered constraints. Cassian felt adrenaline rise.

“Do not accept it yet.”

Imani nodded. They held the prediction.

Pike arranged a new sensor pass against a region of the interface no one had previously reconstructed at useful resolution. No model feedback. No adaptive scanning.

The instrument team was given coordinates but not expected geometry.

Hours later the raw returns arrived.

Cassian refused to look. Imani refused to look.

Nico, demonstrating unexpected discipline, covered the display with a physical jacket.

LOAM compared the new raw data against all six preregistered candidates. Five failed. One survived.

The room became silent. Imani removed the jacket.

The predicted relationship was there, not perfect. Damaged. Noisy. Partial. Enough.

Nico whispered, “Predictive resemblance.” Imani did not correct him. Cassian looked at her. She sat down, not dramatically.

As though her knees had remembered gravity.

“That is the first thing,” she said, “that I cannot explain by us choosing the resemblance after the fact.”

Pike asked, “What does it prove?” Imani recovered immediately.

“That this section of the Antarctic structure contains recoverable relational information and that our model predicted part of it before measurement.”

“Artificial?”

“Stronger.”

“Connected to human antiquity?”

“No.”

Nico groaned. Imani pointed at him.

“Do not skip steps because one step frightened you.”

Cassian saved the result. The archive had passed the first test that mattered.

It had told them something about a place they had not yet looked.

The failed predictions became more important than the successful one.

LOAM generated six candidate reconstructions. Five failed.

Imani forced the team to keep them visible on the wall after the surviving candidate matched the new sensor return. Nico asked why.

“So nobody remembers the experiment as inevitable.”

She highlighted Candidate Two. It had looked strongest to three human reviewers.

Candidate Four had produced the most aesthetically satisfying symmetry.

Candidate Six had matched a Mesopotamian ratio family better than the winner. All wrong. Cassian understood. Memory compressed uncertainty after success.

People remembered the answer and forgot the branching paths that could have led elsewhere. LOAM displayed:

POST-HOC NARRATIVE COMPRESSION DETECTED IN HUMAN DEBRIEF LANGUAGE.

Pike frowned.

“What?”

The first debrief drafts already contained phrases like:

the predicted geometry
the expected structure
the successful decode

LOAM compared them with preregistration language.

One candidate among six. Conditional prediction. Competing models.
