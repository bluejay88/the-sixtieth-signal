CHAPTER FOURTEEN
THE FIRST CHECKSUM

A checksum was not a message.

Cassian repeated that sentence until Nico threatened to put it on merchandise.

“It would sell,” Nico said.

“It would confuse people.”

“That has never hurt merchandise.”

Imani stood at the wall with the old and new scans side by side. The Antarctic structure contained redundancy.

That much they could now defend.

The sexagesimal grouping predicted missing elements visible in an earlier image.

That was stronger than resemblance. Still not language. Still not origin. Still not aliens.

Cassian found comfort in every noun they could not yet use. Pike did not.

“What can a checksum tell us?” she asked.

“That data was structured to survive error,” Cassian said.

“Or that a natural pattern happens to be redundant.”

“Yes.”

“Probability?”

“Higher for engineered structure now.”

“How high?”

LOAM displayed several model classes.

Natural geologic process. Instrumental artifact. Human-made structure. Unknown engineered structure.

The unknown engineered class had risen sharply, not to certainty. Enough.

Nico said, “Unknown engineered is doing a lot of emotional work.” Imani said, “Better than extraterrestrial.”

Pike asked, “Can the checksum recover more?” Cassian nodded.

“Maybe.”

The damaged Antarctic sequence had more missing regions.

Now that they possessed an independently validated grouping rule, LOAM could reconstruct candidates. Imani imposed conditions.

“No semantic labeling.”

“Agreed.”

“No ancient-text comparisons until reconstruction is frozen.”

“Agreed.”

“No choosing among multiple reconstructions because one looks culturally interesting.”

“Agreed.”

Nico raised his hand.

“No narrative satisfaction.”

Imani pointed at him. “Especially that.” LOAM generated six candidate reconstructions.

Only two satisfied all local constraints. Cassian asked for discrimination.

The system identified a later repeated sequence elsewhere in the geometry.

One candidate predicted it. The other did not.

They had a stable mathematical structure. Then something unexpected happened.

The stable structure was not one-dimensional.

Imani rotated the map ninety degrees.

The same values formed another valid relation across rows. Cassian frowned.

“A two-axis checksum.”

“Like a parity grid,” Nico said.

“Conceptually.”

Imani looked at the repeated relations.

“Could be a table.”

Cassian said, “Or error correction.”

“Those aren’t mutually exclusive.”

LOAM projected a grid.

Twelve columns. Five visible row families. Damage obscuring others.

Cassian felt the number before anyone said it. Twelve columns.

If there were five full row blocks— Sixty cells.

Nico whispered, “You have got to be kidding.”

Imani said, “Do not celebrate arithmetic.”

“No promises.”

Pike stared at the grid.

“SVW never reconstructed this.”

“Because they segmented the marks as language,” Imani said.

Cassian understood. They had been trying to read.

The structure might need to be computed first. LOAM filled no cells.

It outlined only what the evidence supported. Twelve columns.

Five rows visible enough to test. Sixty positions.

But some positions contained clusters, not single marks. Nested structure.

“Could be coordinates,” Nico said.

“Could be inventory,” Imani replied.

Cassian said, “Could be protocol.” Pike turned to him.

“Why?”

“Because checksums matter when transmission matters.”

“Storage too.”

“Yes.”

“What does it store?”

“We don’t know.”

Nico pointed at the grid.

“Can we feed it back?”

Pike said, “No.” Cassian said, “Not yet.” She looked at him.

“Not ever without authorization.”

He did not answer. Imani changed the display to the earlier scan. The grid survived.

Then she overlaid a separate acoustic event from 2041. Different modality. Different equipment.
