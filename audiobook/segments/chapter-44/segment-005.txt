By the end of Phase One, thousands of small decisions had become prototypes for the Consent Protocol, not clean philosophical examples.

Grandmothers with three names. Children whose custody records vanished. Patients whose best option was not the safest option. Communities where a red icon became pressure.

The protocol would later use words like agency and divergence.

On the ground those words meant something more ordinary. Who gets to say no?

Who is believed when systems disagree?

Who writes down why an exception happened?

And who comes back afterward to ask whether emergency was telling the truth when it said there was no alternative? The first test came immediately.

A regional medical authority proposed compulsory biometric enrollment so displaced people could regain medication access. Continuity score: high. Agency: poor.

Divergence: poor because one identity infrastructure would become mandatory. Cassian asked:

Manual attestation plus local witness verification.

Slower. Higher fraud. Less centralized. LOAM modeled both.
