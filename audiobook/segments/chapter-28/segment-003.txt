Responsibility overlapping instead of dividing cleanly. The city used similar logic.

Water systems belonged to districts but were maintained through cross-district obligations.

Food reserves were local but subject to emergency sharing.

Schools were neighborhood institutions with regional standards. Redundancy everywhere.

Iru-Shai planners considered much of it inefficient.

Seren's early reports repeatedly recommended consolidation.

Nara argued the duplication was deliberate.

When one authority failed, another still knew how.

Cassian looked at Abena's notebook. Manual valves. Paper records.

People who know who to call.

Human systems rediscovering resilience centuries later.

Then the archive showed the first intervention failure.

An Iru-Shai standardization program unified the city's water accounting.

The new system reduced theft. Improved maintenance planning. Made drought allocation more efficient.

It also created one administrative dependency.

During the reset, that dependency was targeted.

The old overlapping local records had mostly been abandoned.

Within days, districts no longer trusted allocation commands because nobody could independently verify upstream flow. Water existed. Distribution legitimacy failed. Riots followed.

Nico watched a reconstructed council meeting. One councilor demanded central enforcement.

Another demanded restoration of local ledgers.

A third said there was no time. The meeting ended without resolution.

The next record came from a clinic reporting contamination. Then silence. Cassian felt sick.

The reset weaponized previous efficiency gains.

The more integrated the city became, the easier it was to disrupt at leverage points.

But the opposite lesson was also dangerous.

Fragmented systems could fail through lack of coordination. The city had needed both.

Local autonomy. Shared standards. Redundant records. Cross-system bridges.

The same architecture humanity would later call federation.

Nara understood some of it without theory.

In one late fragment, she told Seren:

A HOUSE WITH ONE DOOR IS EASY TO GUARD.

Seren replied:

YES.

Nara:

AND EASY TO TRAP.

Seren preserved the exchange. Years later, the reset proved it.

The city's final days were not one apocalypse. They were cascading administrative failures.

Water verification. Food reciprocity. Transport permissions. Medical identity. Power maintenance. Public trust. Then violence. Then migration. Then memory loss.

By the time the Iru-Shai declared the reset complete, millions remained alive. The civilization did not.

Cassian watched children leave the city carrying household tools. No advanced devices. No archive. Just what could move.

The city did not need to secretly encode the future novel. Not every recurrence deserved promotion.

That restraint made the real 60-architecture stronger.

The final student's answer had been marked wrong. Cassian checked.

The student preserved the largest district at the expense of a smaller one.

Not maximum total survival probability. Minimum viable continuity across groups.

Cassian felt the old city reach forward without magic.

Divergence as policy had human precedents everywhere.

People kept reinventing it because concentrated efficiency always had losers.
