Once communities could no longer verify who had contributed, reciprocity collapsed. Hoarding began. Cassian stared at the sequence.

The reset did not need to destroy food.

It destroyed confidence that cooperation would be reciprocated.

Within weeks, inventories that should have lasted months became inaccessible behind local protection. Nico said:

“So civilization died because nobody trusted the ledger.”

Imani shook her head.

“Not only.”

But the ledger mattered. Cassian thought of the ancient beer tablet. Quantity. Recipient. Transfer. Accountability.

The first information revolution and the last one shared a vulnerability. Trust. Survivors carried stories, not blueprints, not technical manuals. Stories.

A great place before water. A punishment.

A betrayal among powerful beings. A warning about knowledge.

None of those motifs mapped cleanly to any single known myth. Imani insisted on that.

“We are not going to point at one flood story and declare this its source. Human beings live near water. Floods happen. Stories travel. Similarity is not genealogy.”

Nico said, “But the archive says survivors carried memory.”

“Yes. That is evidence inside our fictional event chain. It is not permission to colonize every real tradition with one answer.”

Cassian smiled.

“You rehearsed that.”

“For twenty years.”

The final record from the city was not a warning. It was a shopping list.

LOAM reconstructed quantities for grain, dried fruit, lamp oil, cloth, and something uncertain that may have been fermented drink. Nico stared.

“That's the last thing?”

“Last surviving thing,” Imani corrected.

Cassian looked at the list.

Civilizations wanted to be remembered for temples and kings.

Archives often preserved what people needed on Tuesday.

That was the city beneath memory, not a superhuman race.

Not proof that ancient people required alien teachers. People.

Advanced in some ways. Ordinary in most.

Vulnerable because ordinary life depended on systems they believed would continue.

The same mistake Cassian modeled every day.

LOAM posted the city's final intervention classification.

RESET AUTHORITY: DISPUTED.

The shopping list remained beside it. Oil. Grain. Cloth. Fruit.

The city beneath memory was not beautiful. That saved it. No crystal towers. No impossible anti-gravity machines.

No proof that humanity had once been perfect.

The archive reconstructed streets, storage rooms, homes, workshops, drainage channels, public kitchens. People lived there. That was enough.

Nico kept waiting for the “advanced civilization reveal.” Imani kept refusing him.

“They had sophisticated systems.”

“Advanced.”

“That word hides more than it explains.”

“Compared with contemporaries?”

“In the story universe, yes.”

“Thank you.”

“They also argued about rent.”

Nico stared.

“Seriously?”

Imani showed him a translated administrative dispute. Cassian laughed.

The city became real in fragments.

Grain allocations. Lamp oil. Cloth. Repair schedules. A complaint about noise from a workshop. A request for more public water points.

Then the reset record, not fire from heaven. Systems failure.

Transport disabled. Water coordination broken. Stored knowledge erased. Population movement forced. Leadership removed.

The city died organizationally before it died physically. Cassian felt sick.

This was Shaal-Amur’s method at human scale. Destroy convergence. Leave survivors. History interprets collapse as catastrophe.

The archive showed one family’s ration list from the final week.

Barley. Oil. Dried fruit. Salt. Ordinary things. Nico stopped filming. Cassian asked why.

“Because this part is not content.”

Imani looked at him.

It was the first time she seemed to trust him completely.
