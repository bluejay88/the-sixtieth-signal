The city’s greatest evidence of lost civilization was not a machine.

It was people expecting to eat tomorrow.

The lost city had a school. That detail destroyed Nico.

The archive reconstructed a room with low benches, repeated practice marks, and storage niches containing damaged learning tablets, not advanced machinery. Children learning.

Imani translated one exercise cautiously. Quantity transfer.

A student had made the same mistake three times.

The instructor had corrected it three different ways. Nico stared at the marks.

“They were annoying children.”

Cassian smiled.

“Probably.”

“No, I mean—”

He stopped. The city had been abstract while it was infrastructure. Schools made it temporal.

Adults expected children to need knowledge later.

Civilization was a bet on tomorrow.

The reset destroyed that first, not bodies. Continuity.

Teachers disappeared. Records became unusable. Transport collapsed. Administrative memory fragmented.

The city’s technologies did not need to be more spectacular than ours.

They only needed to be sufficiently interdependent that targeted disruption could make advanced life unrecoverable within a generation. Cassian looked at LOAM’s reconstruction.

“This is what Shaal-Amur calls a reset.”

Imani nodded.

“Not erasure of people.”

“Erasure of accumulated capacity.”

The distinction made the crime larger.

A civilization could survive genetically and still be murdered historically.

Things someone expected to need tomorrow.

Tomorrow never came in the form they planned.
