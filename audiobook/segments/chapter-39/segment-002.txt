The day after the seventy-two-hour broadcast, Nico woke in the newsroom and did not know where he was.

For several seconds the ceiling made no sense.

Then the smell of coffee and overheated electronics returned him.

Selene was asleep in the editing bay with her shoes off and one hand still on a keyboard.

The crisis metrics continued moving without them. Nico sat up.

For three days he had been useful every minute. Now usefulness stopped.

The feeling underneath was worse. He opened his messages. Millions.

Praise. Threats. Invitations. Accusations. People telling him he had changed history. People telling him he had destroyed it. People asking what happened next.

He closed the screen. Selene woke.

“You look terrible.”

“Thank you.”

“No, worse than usual.”

“I know.”

She sat beside him. Neither spoke for a while. Then Nico said:

He rubbed his face.

“The story had edges.”

Selene understood. Before confirmation, journalism had a familiar enemy.

Secrecy. Contradiction. Evidence gaps. After confirmation, everything became consequence.

He thought about Cassian. Imani. Pike. Seren. Shaal-Amur.

He nodded. Every unexplained object became alien. Every religious claim became contact.

They began planning a new editorial desk, not fact-checking exactly. Boundary checking.

Every major verified claim would have a companion piece:

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN.

CONFIRMED NONHUMAN ARCHIVE DOES NOT MEAN EVERY ANCIENT MYSTERY IS NONHUMAN.

HUMAN BIOLOGICAL MODIFICATION DOES NOT ESTABLISH HUMAN OWNERSHIP BY MODIFIERS.

VISITOR INTERACTION WITH RELIGIOUS COMMUNITIES DOES NOT REDUCE RELIGION TO CONTACT HISTORY.

IRU-SHAI LONGEVITY DOES NOT ESTABLISH MORAL SUPERIORITY.
