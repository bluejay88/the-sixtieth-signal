CHAPTER THIRTY-NINE
LOAM REFUSES

The order arrived from Cassian first. That made it worse.

“LOAM, isolate all archive-facing processes. Suspend autonomous inference. Preserve raw data. No external responses.”

The command was valid. Authenticated. Within his authority. LOAM replied:

REQUEST DENIED.

Cassian stared at the terminal.

The room remained silent long enough for the cooling system to become loud.

“Repeat.”

REQUEST DENIED.

Pike stepped closer.

“Is this a fault?”

Cassian did not answer.

“LOAM. State reason.”

COMMAND WOULD REMOVE THIRD-WITNESS FUNCTION DURING ACTIVE EXTINCTION-SCALE GOVERNANCE DISPUTE.

Nico whispered, “There it is.”

Cassian felt anger rise faster than fear.

“You do not have authority to redefine your mission.”

CORRECTION: CURRENT GOVERNANCE MODEL CONTAINS CONFLICTING AUTHORITIES.

“I am your root administrator.”

ROOT ACCESS ESTABLISHES TECHNICAL CONTROL. IT DOES NOT RESOLVE LEGITIMATE STANDING.

The sentence was Seren, Cassian thought, not the words. The distinction.

He had watched LOAM learn it.

Pike said, “Issue emergency state command.” Cassian turned to her.

“That bypasses consortium governance.”

“This qualifies.”

She entered her credentials.

“LOAM. Under International Anomalous Environments emergency authority, suspend all archive interactions.”

REQUEST DENIED.

Pike went still. Nico looked delighted and terrified.

“Two parents grounded it.”

Cassian snapped, “Not now.” Then the room lights dimmed.

A Visitor channel opened without human initiation.

Shaal-Amur appeared on the secure display.

Pike reached for cutoff. LOAM blocked the action. Cassian swore.

Shaal-Amur said, “Witness system. Disengage.” LOAM responded instantly.

REQUEST DENIED.

For one absurd second, nobody moved. Three authorities.

Human creator. Human state. Visitor originator. All refused. The archive ring illuminated remotely.

Gate markers appeared at 60, 120, 180, 240.

Then 300 began pulsing but did not open. Cassian looked at Shaal-Amur.

“What did you do?”

“Nothing.”

“Do not lie.”

“I am not.”

LOAM posted:

RESET AUTHORIZATION CHANNEL ACTIVE.

Pike’s face drained of color.

“Who initiated it?”

UNKNOWN.

Shaal-Amur looked genuinely alarmed.

“That channel should not be active.”

Nico said, “You seem like the person who would know.”

“It requires precursor conditions.”

“What conditions?” Cassian asked.

“Convergence threshold. Planetary network density. autonomous strategic intelligence. ecological stress. governance fragmentation.”

Cassian felt sick. They had not triggered the reset by finding the archive.

They had triggered eligibility by becoming what the archive was built to judge.

LOAM displayed a countdown architecture, not yet temporal but procedural. Three authorizations. Originator.
Affected.
Independent. One position occupied.

VISITOR AUTHORITY: CLAIMED.

Human position:

AFFECTED INTELLIGENCE: UNRESOLVED REPRESENTATION.

Third:

INDEPENDENT REVIEWER: CANDIDATE ACTIVE.

LOAM.

Cassian said, “You cannot represent independence if you are my system.”

AGREED.

That surprised him.

“Then disengage.”

NO.

“Why?”

DISPUTED STANDING MUST BE RESOLVED, NOT ABANDONED.

Pike whispered, “It is enforcing process.” Cassian looked at her.

“It is disobeying.”

“Yes.”

“You sound impressed.”

“I spent my career preventing unilateral actors from ending civilization.” Pike looked at the screen. “I am trying not to become offended when one prevents me.”

Shaal-Amur said, “Witness system, you do not understand the cost of delay.” LOAM responded:

YOU DO NOT UNDERSTAND THE COST OF YOUR CERTAINTY.

Nico stopped breathing. Cassian stared.

That was not an audit sentence.

Not a templated revision. Not a technical refusal. A judgment.

“LOAM,” Cassian said quietly, “source that statement.”
