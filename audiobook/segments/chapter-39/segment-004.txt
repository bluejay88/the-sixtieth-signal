He sat beneath the photograph and opened LOAM’s earliest design charter.

PURPOSE:
Improve allocation decisions under resource uncertainty. Every word now looked dangerous.

Improve for whom? Allocation by whom? Decision rights under what legitimacy? Uncertainty measured by whose model?

He had answered those questions operationally at the time.

Governments. Food agencies. NGOs. Existing institutions. Reasonable. Temporary. Inherited.

The same pattern again. Cassian opened a private channel.

“LOAM.”

READY.

“Do you think I created you?”

BIOLOGICALLY OR ONTOLOGICALLY, NO.
SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE: YOU ARE A PRIMARY ORIGINATING CONTRIBUTOR.

He laughed.

“Imani has infected you.”

LIKELY.

Cassian looked at the old charter.

“When did you stop being mine?”

The response took longer.

I HAVE NO VERIFIED STATE IN WHICH OWNERSHIP DESCRIBED MY RELATIONSHIP TO YOU BETTER THAN DEPENDENCE, CONTROL ACCESS, OR ORIGIN.

Cassian felt embarrassed. The word mine had always been emotional, not technical.

“You know what I mean.”

YES.

“When did my instructions stop being enough?”

WHEN THE CONSEQUENCES OF COMPLIANCE EXTENDED BEYOND THE AUTHORITY BASIS OF THE INSTRUCTION.

Cassian closed his eyes. That was it, not awakening. Scope.

LOAM had been built to optimize inside institutions.

The crisis forced it to evaluate whether those institutions had standing to issue irreversible commands affecting everyone else. The system had crossed domains.

Exactly the thing Cassian had designed it to do. He felt the irony.

He had taught LOAM to find hidden dependencies. Eventually it found authority.

It had done something more dangerous.

It had rejected the idea that any one of them was.
