Imani folded her arms. “Good. Now ruin the beautiful version.” LOAM listed alternatives.

Archive timestamp error. Unknown relay behavior. Predictive reconstruction by the sender. Information leakage through an unidentified human or Iru-Shai channel. A closed causal relationship. Five possibilities.

No percentages high enough to satisfy anyone. Cassian felt relief.

Not because the mystery had become smaller. Because it still had competitors.

He opened the first anomaly report. The sentence at the bottom remained where he had left it years earlier:

RECOMMENDATION: DO NOT EXCLUDE THE VARIABLE.

Nico read over his shoulder.

“That sentence has had an unreasonable career.”

“It was a model note.”

“Now it's a religion.”

Imani turned. “Do not.” Nico raised both hands. “A joke.”

“That is how bad nouns enter public life.”

“Also a joke.”

Cassian sat at the workstation. “What do we do with the hypothesis?” LOAM answered before Nico could.

TEST IT.

This time Cassian smiled. Outside the laboratory, the world had not become enlightened in any useful final sense. Food networks were slower and more redundant. Emergency systems carried more paper and more local fallback authority. Governments argued over the Consent Protocol. Courts disagreed about machine refusal. Iru-Shai constitutional proceedings continued. Some religious communities had absorbed the discovery; others had rejected the categories imposed by it. Markets had recovered unevenly. People still lied. Systems still failed.

The difference was that more of the failure paths were visible.

Abena called while Cassian was reading the new test plan. He answered.

“Are you eating?”

“Hello to you too.”

“That means no.”

“I had lunch.”

“What?”

Cassian glanced at Imani. She mouthed: Do not involve me.

“Food.”

Abena stared through the screen. Nico whispered, “Strong evidence.” Cassian muted him.

Abena had spent the year turning the community center's emergency improvisations into boring procedures: generator tests, cooling agreements, printed contacts, water-storage rotations, radio drills. The chipped yellow cup was still there too, now holding pens because somebody had finally decided the crack was not worth trusting with hot coffee. She treated every consultant who used the word resilience too often as a personal enemy.

“They sent the school forms again,” she said.

“What forms?”

“The emergency drill.”

“Is something wrong with them?”

“The font.”

Cassian closed his eyes.

“Civilization survives the reset and you are fighting typography.”

“Bad typography survives everything.”

Imani laughed loud enough for Abena to hear.

“There she is,” Abena said. “At least one intelligent person is with you.”

Cassian looked toward the signal comparison still glowing on the wall.

For most of his life he had believed uncertainty was a deficit to be reduced. Sometimes it was. A wrong medication dose did not become profound because nobody knew the answer. A failing cold chain did not need philosophy. It needed repair. But he could see now how often he had used analysis to turn fear into a work queue. Give the unknown a variable name. Put it on a map. Make the map precise enough and maybe the people he loved could not fall through it.

But uncertainty could also mark a boundary around claims nobody had earned yet.

That distinction had taken him years.

After the call, Nico packed one recorder and left two.

“Growth,” Imani said.

“Budget cuts.”

They walked outside together. The city carried traces of the crisis in ordinary places: manual water points beside networked ones, paper notices next to public displays, emergency radio frequencies posted at transit stations. None of it looked like a new age. Cassian preferred that.

Imani stopped at the edge of the plaza.
