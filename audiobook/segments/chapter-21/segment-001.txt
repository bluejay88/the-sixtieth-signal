PART III
THE HALF TURN

CHAPTER TWENTY-ONE
THE GOVERNMENT ALREADY KNEW

Maelin Pike waited until the Antarctic feed had been severed from every network except hers before she told them the truth.

Not the truth about what was under the ice.

The truth about how long people had been failing to understand it.

The conference room beneath the Geneva food-security annex was windowless by design. Cassian had assumed that was because it doubled as a continuity facility. Then Pike opened a wall that was not a wall.

Behind it were shelves. Paper.

Thousands of folders in climate-controlled rows.

Nico stared at them with something approaching reverence.

“You kept this offline.”

“Much of it predates reliable digital custody,” Pike said. “The rest is offline because every network eventually becomes someone else's evidence.”

Imani ran a finger along the nearest spine without touching it.

SOUTHERN VARIANCE WATCH - 2029.

The date made Cassian stop.

“That was before LOAM existed.”

Pike looked at him.

“We have been watching it longer than you've been alive.”

Nico's head snapped toward her.

“You said the program began as geophysics.”

“It did.”

“And when did it stop being geophysics?”

“It never did. That's one of the mistakes people make when they learn a secret. They assume the old explanation disappears. It doesn't. The ice is still ice. The brine is still brine. The gravity residual is still a gravity residual. We simply acquired more questions than one discipline could answer.”

She took down three binders.

The first contained photographs of borehole cores. Nothing artificial.

The second contained acoustic returns that looked uncomfortably like the geometry beneath coordinate three. The third contained competing interpretations.

WEAPON PLATFORM.

NONHUMAN SENSOR ARRAY.

PALEOGEOLOGIC CAVITY.

FOREIGN STATE INSTALLATION.

UNKNOWN METAMATERIAL DEPOSIT.

COGNITIVE HAZARD PROTOCOL.

Cassian looked up.

“Cognitive hazard?”

Pike's expression did not change.

“A team in 2057 became convinced the interval patterns were instructions. They altered experiments to satisfy the instructions. Their evidence improved as their controls worsened.”

“Did anyone die?” Imani asked.

“No.”

“Then why hazard?”

“Because intelligence can be injured without blood.”

Pike slid a report across the table. Six researchers.

Three later retracted their findings. One left science. Two continued publishing increasingly elaborate mathematical correspondences between the Antarctic data and ancient systems of measure.

Nico read the title of one paper.

“Sexagesimal Echoes in Deep-Time Signal Architecture.”

Imani said, “Oh, absolutely not.”

“You haven't read it.”

“I don't need to. The title already owes me an apology.”

Pike almost smiled.

“That was the institutional reaction. We shut down interpretive work and restricted analysis to preregistered physical tests.”

Cassian understood the shape of the failure.

The anomaly had not been hidden because governments possessed a clean answer.

It had been hidden because they had produced too many.

“How many countries know?”

“Know what?”

“That something structured exists.”

“Depending on your standard? Seven governments. Twelve scientific agencies. Three military alliances. A handful of contractors.”

Nico gave a dry laugh.

“That's not a secret. That's a conference.”

“Correct. Which is why there have been leaks for thirty years. None survived because the leaks contradicted one another.”

Pike projected a timeline. An alleged alien beacon in 2049.

A secret Chinese thermal weapon in 2052.

An ancient city in 2058. A volcanic superstructure in 2060.

A machine under the ice in 2062.

A hoax in 2063 proving all previous claims were fabricated.
