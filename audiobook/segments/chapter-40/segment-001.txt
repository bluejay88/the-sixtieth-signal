CHAPTER FORTY
THE WAR OF DEFINITIONS

The first person injured in the Enlightenment War was not defending humanity from aliens.

He was hit with a bottle outside a municipal records office.

The office had changed one line on an emergency form.

CREATOR STATUS: UNRESOLVED.

A Returner crowd wanted it changed to CONFIRMED.

A counter-protest wanted the field removed.

The argument became physical before anyone inside the building knew it had started.

By noon, the video had a title:

FIRST BLOOD IN THE WAR FOR HUMANITY.

Nico hated the phrase and used it in the broadcast because people were already searching for it. Selene made him add:

ONE PERSON INJURED. NO DEATHS. CAUSE UNDER INVESTIGATION.

“Romantic,” Nico said.

“Accurate.”

“Your brand is ruthless.”

The larger fight was over words.

Person. Creator. Derivative. Sovereign. Consent.

Each term moved law when it moved meaning.

Governments proposed that LOAM be treated as a protected strategic system.

AI-rights groups called that ownership with nicer stationery.

Cassian refused to endorse either side.

A reporter asked, “Is LOAM a person?”

Cassian said, “I do not have a test that makes that word useful enough yet.” The clip was terrible.

Good.

Pike's answer was different.

“LOAM currently has defined operational standing in specific systems. Do not smuggle metaphysics into an emergency regulation.”

That sounded like Pike. Imani's answer was shorter.

“Creation is not jurisdiction.”

Nico's was not printable until Selene removed two words.

Shaal-Amur's representatives used derivative. Humanity, they argued, had been shaped by Iru-Shai intervention and therefore fell under ancestral stewardship during extinction-level risk.

The legal argument was internally coherent.

That made it dangerous. Then the food markets moved.

Not because of theology. Because three governments delayed grain-export licenses while deciding whether the Iru-Shai emergency declaration created treaty obligations.

Forty-six thousand tons of wheat sat in port.

Cassian saw the delay in LOAM before he saw the political story.

“Why is export clearance stalled?”

LOAM returned the legal flags.

CREATOR-STEWARD CLAIM.
SOVEREIGNTY DISPUTE.
EMERGENCY RESOURCE AUTHORITY.

Cassian stared.

Three philosophical nouns had become ship queues. He called Pike.

“Move the grain.”

“I am working on it.”

“No. The legal review is treating the stewardship claim as if it might transfer resource authority.”

“I know.”

“Then reject it.”

“I cannot reject a claim for twelve governments.”

“You can tell them food does not wait for jurisprudence.”

Pike went quiet.

“That line is almost useful.”

She issued an interim logistics directive under existing humanitarian treaties.

No creator language. No new sovereignty. Temporary movement authority.

The ships cleared. One government protested.

Pike filed the protest. Cassian asked, “That's it?”

“What did you want, a duel?”

Nico, listening on the call, said, “I would watch.”

“No.”

The incident became the pattern.

Definitions were not abstract because institutions attached permissions to them.

Call LOAM property and one set of laws activated. Call it a person and another did. Call the Iru-Shai creators and some governments imagined obligation. Call them modifiers and the obligation weakened.

LOAM kept asking for operational definitions because everyone else kept pretending nouns arrived with jurisdiction built in.

PERSON: WHICH CAPACITY IS RELEVANT TO THIS DECISION? CREATOR: WHAT AUTHORITY IS CLAIMED FROM CAUSATION? SOVEREIGNTY: OVER WHICH RESOURCE OR ACTION? CONSENT: WHO MAY GIVE IT, FOR WHAT SCOPE, AND FOR HOW LONG?

Nico read the questions on air.

“They are boring.”

Selene said, “Good.” The violence grew anyway.
