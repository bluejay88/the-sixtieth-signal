CHAPTER THIRTY-TWO
THE FIRST PUBLIC PROOF

The leak was six minutes long. That was enough.

No face appeared in it. No alien body. No government confession. Only three things.

A time-synchronized raw acoustic trace from Antarctica.

An independent instrument log showing the same structured response.

And a twenty-two-second video of Imani Sayegh saying, with visible irritation, “I am not telling you this is extraterrestrial. I am telling you the structure is not adequately explained by the geology we have tested.”

The clip reached forty million verified views in eighteen minutes.

By the time Pike’s office issued its first statement, there were already nine altered versions in circulation.

One added a figure standing behind Imani.

One replaced the acoustic trace with whale song.

One inserted subtitles claiming the signal translated to kneel.

One was better than the original.

That frightened Nico more than any of them.

At 19:12 UTC, the leak reached a grain terminal in Tema.

Not as evidence. As rumor.

A forged subtitle claimed the Antarctic signal warned of imminent crop failure. Drivers abandoned two loading queues to call family. A contractor suspended an overnight shift. By the time the fake was corrected, forty-seven refrigerated containers had missed rail transfer and a school-nutrition shipment was six hours behind schedule.

Cassian saw the logistics exception before Nico saw the rumor. He called him.

“Your fake alien prophecy is now delaying lentils.”

Nico blinked. “That sentence has too much going on.”

“Can you kill it?”

“No. I can make the correction easier to find than the lie for the people who matter.”

“Do that.”

Nico and Selene pushed the correction directly through the logistics unions, port channels, local radio, and the terminal's own messaging groups instead of leading with another global broadcast.

The night shift returned, not all of it. The shipment moved.

Cassian added the incident to the disclosure model.

INFORMATION ERROR -> LABOR RESPONSE -> LOGISTICS DELAY -> FOOD ACCESS.

The archive had become part of the famine model without changing a single crop.

He sat in a secure room three levels above the archive access station, watching authenticity dissolve in real time.

“People keep asking me which video is real,” he said.

Pike stood at the far table with three officials on encrypted calls.

“Tell them none are verified.”

“The original is verified.”

“Not publicly.”

Nico turned.

“That distinction has approximately seven minutes left to live.”

Pike ended one call and started another. Cassian entered with Imani.

“They’re cloning LOAM’s visualization style,” Cassian said.

Nico looked at him. “Of course they are.”

“How?”

“Your presentations have been public for years.”

A new clip appeared on Nico’s monitor.

Black background. White causal graphs. Amber Antarctic point. Then text:

LOAM CONFIRMS NONHUMAN ORIGIN.

Cassian swore.

“It never said that.”

“Doesn’t matter.”

“It matters enormously.”

“To truth. Not to virality.”

Imani watched the feed without expression.

A religious broadcaster declared the structure proof of angels.

A secular channel declared it proof religions had always been fraudulent.

A famous physicist called the evidence “suggestive but radically incomplete.”

His quote was reposted as PHYSICIST CONFIRMS ALIEN VAULT.

A market analyst blamed the leak for a commodities selloff that had actually begun forty minutes earlier. Three governments denied knowledge.

Two admitted participation in Antarctic monitoring.

One claimed the footage was generated.

Pike said, “We need a verification release.” Nico stared at her.

“You have become radicalized.”

“A limited one.”

“Still counts.”

Cassian sat beside him.

“What can we prove?”

Imani answered.
