Outside, wind pushed loose ice grains against the logistics module like dry sand. Cassian watched her work.

For the first time since the response beneath the ice, he felt the investigation acquire a shape he trusted. Not belief.

Rules for how belief would be earned.

Imani's first argument was not with Nico.

It was with Professor Rafi Dalan.

He appeared on a secure call from Leiden wearing an old sweater and the expression of a man whose former student had just sent him something professionally dangerous.

“You are pattern-matching,” he said.

Imani did not flinch.

“Possibly.”

Cassian watched from the corner. Dalan enlarged the Antarctic marks.

“You have damaged material, unknown orientation, unknown sign inventory, unknown medium conventions, unknown chronology, and a machine capable of exploring transformation space faster than any human scholar can audit.”

“Yes.”

“And you are excited.”

“No.”

“You are lying.”

Imani smiled despite herself. Dalan had supervised her doctoral work on administrative sign systems.

He knew exactly how she looked when frightened by something interesting.

“What would satisfy you?” she asked.

“Not resemblance.”

“Agreed.”

“Not base sixty by itself. Mesopotamia used sexagesimal mathematics. That does not make every sixty a telegram.”

“Agreed.”

“Not flood stories.”

“Agreed.”

“Not one spectacular translation.”

“Especially agreed.”

Dalan leaned closer to the camera.

“Prediction.”

Cassian looked up. Imani said, “Independent.”

“Yes. Something derived from the Antarctic material that predicts a feature in an unopened source or unmeasured section.”

“That is already our threshold.”

Dalan's face softened.

“Good.”

Then hardened again.

“And publish your failures.”

Pike, listening remotely, said:

“Absolutely not.”

Dalan looked toward the source of the voice.

“Then you do not want scholarship. You want confidence.”

Pike replied, “I want operational security.”

“Those are not mutually exclusive until someone makes them so.”

The call became an argument about embargoed replication, redacted methods, and whether a classified discovery could survive ordinary peer criticism. Imani listened.

Cassian realized the real institutional conflict had arrived.

Not scientist versus conspiracy theorist. Science versus secrecy. Dalan finally said:

“If you cannot show the world every result yet, then at minimum preserve the negative results with the same care as the positive ones.”

Imani looked at Pike. Pike looked at Cassian. LOAM added:

RECOMMENDATION: FAILURE ARCHIVE.

Nico whispered, “That is a terrible podcast name.” Imani opened a new repository.

FAILED PARSES.
FAILED ALIGNMENTS.
FAILED HISTORICAL MATCHES.
FAILED BIOLOGICAL CORRELATIONS.

Every dead end received provenance.

If the conspiracy was real, it would have to survive a record of all the ways it failed to become one. She looked at Cassian.

“This looks like something humans might have borrowed from.”
