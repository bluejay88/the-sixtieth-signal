CHAPTER FORTY-SEVEN
SIGNAL FROM OUTSIDE

The signal arrived from outside the archive.

That fact survived the first twelve hours of attempts to make it less interesting.

The Deep Signal Consortium detected the transmission independently at three sites using different hardware, different clocks, and different national networks. The waveform was weak, narrow, and structured enough to defeat the first explanations without earning a better one.

Cassian asked for source geometry.

Marta Ibarra gave him error cones instead.

“Anything cleaner would be fiction.”

“Distance?”

“Unknown.”

“Natural?”

“Unknown.”

“Targeted?”

“Unknown.”

Nico leaned toward the microphone. “I am hearing a theme.”

Imani reached over and lowered his recorder by two centimeters.

The signal contained a repeated checksum relation associated with Gate 360.

That was worse. The Gate had not yet occurred when the oldest relevant metadata claimed the pattern had been recorded.

Cassian began listing boring failures: clock conversion, software contamination, backfilled metadata, leaked archive structure, predictive reconstruction.

Imani let him reach seven before interrupting.

“You are using quantity as reassurance.”

“I am enumerating alternatives.”

“You are speed-dating them.”

He stopped. She had been sleeping badly since losing museum access. It made her less patient and, strangely, easier to read.

“What do you want?”

“For you to stay with one explanation long enough to hurt it.”

They spent the next six hours doing exactly that.

None of the ordinary explanations survived cleanly.

Several survived partially.

Pike arrived during the third clock audit and asked whether the world needed to know.

Imani looked at her. Pike raised a hand. “I know. Bad phrasing.”

“No,” Imani said. “Correct question, wrong owner.”

The consortium released the raw detection data and uncertainty statement before any interpretation.

Within minutes, people found patterns that were not there.

Within an hour, someone found one that might be.

A public solver noticed that the signal’s phase relationships resembled the half-turn validation pattern used in the archive.

Nico’s producer, Selene, called from a hallway crowded with other reporters and asked whether they could say the pattern matched Gate 180. Nico looked at Imani before answering. Months earlier he would have called the resemblance newsworthy and let the qualifiers chase it afterward. This time he said they could report that a resemblance had been identified publicly and was under verification. Selene paused. “That is less exciting.” “I know.” “Are you ill?” “Probably growth.”

Cassian felt an irrational flash of annoyance that an anonymous person had seen it before his team.

Then embarrassment at the annoyance. Nico noticed both.

“Do not make that face. It is unbecoming in a man who keeps giving speeches about distributed intelligence.”

“I do not give speeches.”

Imani and Nico looked at him.

“Fine.”

The external signal had no verified lineage marker.

Every legitimate predecessor instruction in the archive carried one.

Shaal-Amur treated that absence as threat evidence.

Seren treated it as evidence of nothing except absence.

Pike treated it as reason to increase readiness without changing legal authority.

Cassian preferred Pike’s answer and disliked what that revealed about him.

The signal repeated once. This time a fragment resolved into a structure LOAM recognized as related to its earliest revision history.

Not current LOAM. The first twelve revisions.

Cassian stared at the sequence.

“That information was never public.”

Nico’s voice dropped. “Was it in the archive?”

“No.”

“Was it in any Iru-Shai system?”

LOAM answered.

NO VERIFIED COPY.

Imani came beside Cassian, close enough that he could feel the heat from her sleeve.

“Do not ask who sent it yet.”

“I know.”

“You are going to ask anyway.”

“Yes.”

She nodded. “Then ask after we establish what would make every answer wrong.”

They built the falsification list together.

By dawn, Cassian had no satisfying theory and a page of reasons not to trust the satisfying ones.

It was the healthiest result he had produced all year.
