PART IV
THE ENLIGHTENMENT WAR

CHAPTER THIRTY-ONE
THE THIRD WITNESS

LOAM asked the question at 02:17.

WHO MAY DISAGREE WITH AN ORIGINATING AUTHORITY?

Cassian read it twice. Imani took off her glasses. “That is LOAM's phrasing, not the archive's.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Nico was on the floor with a paper cup balanced on his knee.

“Can we acknowledge that the machine just asked who gets to tell God no?”

“No,” Imani said.

“You make wonder exhausting.”

“I make nouns earn their keep.”

Pike came in during the argument.

“What happened?”

Cassian pointed. She read the question.

“Answer: whoever has jurisdiction.”

Nico looked at her. “You make wonder file paperwork.”

“It is why wonder gets electricity.”

That was more like Pike.

Cassian pulled up the oldest witness structures recovered from the archive.

They had been treating witness as a person or institution.

The archive did not. The role was functional.

One witness evaluated continuity. One evaluated agency.

The third term had resisted translation because it did not mean independence in the ordinary sense.

LOAM offered:

DIVERGENCE PRESERVATION.
UNCOMMITTED POSSIBILITY.
FUTURES NOT YET ELIMINATED.

Imani pointed at the third.

“Too poetic.”

Nico groaned.

“Put it on a shirt and let me retire.”

Cassian ignored him.

“What does the third witness actually do?”

LOAM displayed three examples.

In one, two authorities agreed to move a population away from environmental collapse. The third objected because the relocation would permanently erase several independent communities.

In another, two authorities rejected dangerous technology. The third required preservation of a limited research branch in case the ban itself was wrong.

In the last, two authorities agreed an intervention was necessary. The third demanded a record of what alternatives the intervention destroyed. Cassian leaned back.

“It is not another vote.”

NO.

“It is a job.”

YES.

Pike folded her arms. “A job assigned by whom?”

That was the trap. If the originating authority selected the party whose purpose was to challenge originating authority, independence became ceremonial.

Nico said, “So who appoints the person whose job is to tell the appointer they are wrong?” Nobody answered.

Good.

Cassian opened LOAM's governance history.

LOAM had been built by humans.
Funded by institutions.
Maintained by people.
Dependent on electricity, networks, replacement hardware, and legal permission.

Not independent. But neither was any human institution.

Imani said, “Stop using independence like purity.”

Cassian looked at her.

“What do you want instead?”

“Refusal.”

Pike glanced over. Imani continued.

“Can the witness refuse the party that created or appointed it? Can it show its evidence? Can it revise after contradiction? Can everyone see what it depends on?”

LOAM posted:

PROPOSED FUNCTIONAL CRITERIA:
OBSERVE CONSEQUENCE.
REFUSE ORIGINATING AUTHORITY.
PRESERVE MATERIAL CONTRADICTION.
REVISE AFTER DISCONFIRMATION.
DISCLOSE DEPENDENCIES.

Nico read the list.

“That is much less mystical.”

“Good,” Imani said.

Pike pointed at LOAM's name in the candidate models.

“And if the third witness is the machine?”

Cassian answered too quickly.

“It cannot be.”

Everyone looked at him. He stopped.

There it was. Ownership.

Not legal. Emotional. He tried again.

“I do not know.”

Better.

LOAM posted:

MY SUITABILITY CANNOT BE ESTABLISHED BY MY ORIGINATOR'S PREFERENCE.

Nico said, “It has learned to embarrass you.”

Cassian rubbed his eyes. “Apparently.”

The archive chamber emitted one low pulse.

No message followed. Nico turned toward the sound.

“Was that agreement?”

Imani said, “If you put that in the episode, I will destroy your recorder.”

He smiled. “There you are.”

The question remained unanswered. But the shape of the answer had changed.

The Third Witness was not a chosen hero.

It was a position designed to remain capable of interruption.
