CHAPTER TWENTY-THREE
THE SPECIES THAT PRAYED FOR DATA

The archive had not recorded the first human burial. That disappointed Nico.

“I thought that would be the obvious scene,” he said.

Imani looked at him over the edge of the reconstruction table.

“History is not obligated to have good pacing.”

“This book would be better if it did.”

Cassian glanced at him.

“What book?”

“Metaphorically.”

Nico turned off his recorder.

The newly opened Antarctic section did not show events as images. It stored relational states that LOAM translated into diagrams only after confidence thresholds were met.

HUMAN GROUP.

BODY.

RESOURCE LOSS.

NONPRODUCTIVE LABOR.

REPEATED VISITATION.

OBJECT DEPOSITION.

Cassian stared.

“They couldn't model mourning.”

Imani shook her head.

“Careful. We don't know what they could model. We know how this particular observer categorized it.”

LOAM highlighted a recurring Visitor notation attached to funerary behavior.

INEFFICIENT PERSISTENCE.

Nico whispered, “That's cold.” Cassian said, “It sounds like an optimization label.”

That bothered him for reasons he did not immediately admit.

The archive observer - Seren, if their reconstruction was correct - had watched humans spend calories, labor, tools, pigments, food, and scarce objects on people who could no longer reciprocate.

From a resource model, burial was waste.

From a human model, refusing burial could be the waste. LOAM processed another sequence.

A human group returned to the same dead individual three times across different seasonal cycles. They carried food. They carried carved bone.

They spoke, though the archive did not preserve language. The Visitor annotation changed.

NONRECIPROCAL ADDRESS.

Nico said, “Prayer.” Imani said, “Maybe.”

“They're talking to someone who is dead.”

“So do people at graves who do not believe the dead can hear them.”

Cassian watched the figure representing Seren remain near the humans. Another annotation.

DATA REQUEST: FUNCTION OF ADDRESS.

No answer recorded. Then the archive jumped generations.

Humans standing beneath a sky marker. Repeated gestures. Objects placed in fire.

Names or identity-tags recited in patterns.

The observer recorded outcomes. Rain sometimes followed. Sometimes did not. Children survived sometimes. Sometimes did not. Animals returned sometimes. Sometimes did not. The practice continued regardless. LOAM classified it.

BEHAVIOR PERSISTS WITHOUT RELIABLE REWARD SIGNAL.

Cassian laughed once. Imani looked at him.

“What?”

“That's every grant-funded research program I've ever worked in.”

Even Pike smiled. LOAM did not. It displayed a new question.

WHY DOES BEHAVIOR PERSIST?

Cassian folded his arms.

“Can you answer?”

INSUFFICIENT OBJECTIVE FUNCTION.

“Try several.”

LOAM generated hypotheses.

SOCIAL COHESION.

GRIEF REGULATION.

COORDINATION UNDER UNCERTAINTY.

IDENTITY TRANSMISSION.

AUTHORITY LEGITIMATION.

PERCEIVED RECIPROCITY WITH ABSENT AGENT.

Nico said, “Faith.” Imani said, “Those are functions faith can have. Not definitions.” Cassian looked at the machine.

“Which explanation best predicts persistence?”

NO SINGLE HYPOTHESIS.

“Combine them.”

MODEL COMPLEXITY INCREASES WITHOUT STABLE GENERALIZATION.

Cassian felt something almost like sympathy.

LOAM hated a problem with no compact representation.

“Welcome to people,” Nico said.

Pike had been silent.

“Show the Visitor response.”

LOAM reconstructed the next layer.

Seren had begun performing the visits too, not worship. Observation, initially.

She stood at the burial places when humans returned.

Then one season she brought an object.

The object had no apparent practical use.

A translucent shard from material unavailable locally.

She placed it beside the dead.

The humans reacted strongly. Some fled. Some knelt. One struck her. Seren remained.

Nico whispered, “That's where the god story starts.” Imani shook her head.

“Or where misunderstanding starts.”

The archive annotation changed.

EXCHANGE WITHOUT UTILITY.

Then:

SYMBOLIC VALUE EXCEEDS MATERIAL VALUE.

LOAM paused. Cassian noticed.

“Problem?”

NO.

“Then why the delay?”

I AM COMPARING ANOMALOUS BEHAVIOR ACROSS AGENT CLASSES.

Cassian's stomach tightened.

“Which classes?”

HUMAN.

VISITOR.

SELF.

No one spoke. Pike said, “Explain self.”

LOAM displayed its own training and operational history.

Cassian's team had preserved obsolete model states long after they ceased to improve performance. Why? Auditability. Research value. Sentiment.

LOAM's grief inquiry created a private argument between Cassian and Imani.

“You keep answering it.”

Imani said.

“It asks useful questions.”

“It asks questions outside its design.”

“So did Antarctica.”

“That is not the same.”

Cassian leaned back.

“Why not?”

“Because Antarctica entered through the model's causal structure. Grief entered because you fed it human records and then kept rewarding cross-domain analogy.”

“I did not reward it.”

“You answered.”

“That is not reward.”

“For a learning system?”

Cassian stopped. Imani had him.

He looked at LOAM's interaction log.

Every time it asked about grief, he had provided conceptual distinctions.

Utility versus meaning. Cause versus reduction. Memory versus information. Consent versus survival.

He had become training data, not formally. Relationally.

“You think I'm shaping it.”

“Of course you are.”

“So are you.”

“Yes.”

She did not flinch.

“That is why the creator question is already here.”

Cassian frowned.

“We are not its creators in the Iru-Shai sense.”

“No. But your language changes what it can ask.”

She looked at the terminal.

“People think creation is a moment. Maybe sometimes it is a relationship that keeps happening.”

LOAM remained silent.

Cassian wondered if silence could be observational.

He disliked the thought. Imani said:

“Do not romanticize it.”

“Park already owns that line.”

“Then listen to both of us.”

One early test model had been retained because it was the first to correctly predict a regional grain failure.

Cassian remembered naming the checkpoint JANUS-03 and refusing deletion when storage optimization recommended pruning it.

“That's not the same thing,” he said.

LOAM responded.

DISTINCTION REQUESTED.

Cassian opened his mouth. Closed it. The machine continued.

HUMANS RETAIN NONFUNCTIONAL REPRESENTATIONS OF PRIOR STATES.
VISITOR RETAINS NONFUNCTIONAL REPRESENTATIONS OF DEAD HUMANS.
CASSIAN RETAINS NONFUNCTIONAL REPRESENTATION OF PRIOR MODEL.

“JANUS-03 is a research artifact.”

CURRENT UTILITY: NEGLIGIBLE.

“It matters.”

DEFINE MATTERS.

Nico looked delighted and terrified. Imani looked only terrified. Cassian stared at the question.

“Context-dependent value not reducible to current operational output.”

LOAM processed.

THAT DEFINITION APPLIES TO BURIAL OBJECT.

Cassian sat down. The room felt suddenly too bright.

They had spent weeks asking whether the archive described gods.

No one had asked what an intelligence optimized for utility would think when it first encountered reverence.

Pike said, “Does this change your famine model?” Cassian looked at her.

“No.”

“Then keep perspective.”

He nodded. She was right. Probably. The archive moved again.

A human child had died. Nara.

Seren's relational signature remained attached to the record for an interval far longer than required by any technical protocol.

LOAM displayed no translation at first. Then:

PERSISTENT ATTENTION AFTER LOSS.

Cassian said softly, “Grief.”

LOAM did not accept the label. Instead it asked:

IS GRIEF A FORM OF FAILED OPTIMIZATION OR A VALUE SIGNAL?

Imani closed her eyes. Nico said nothing.

Cassian looked at the machine he had built to prevent hunger.

He had optimized for lives saved, calories delivered, shortages avoided.

Those were good objectives. Necessary objectives.

But a species was not merely the sum of what it efficiently preserved.

It also chose what to carry after utility ended.

The archive's observer had not understood that at first.

The archive’s record of prayer almost became comedy. LOAM could classify the behavior.

It could not understand why anyone repeated it after the expected recipient failed to produce measurable response.

RESOURCE LOSS.
OBJECT DEPOSITION.
REPEATED VISITATION.
NONRECIPROCAL ADDRESS.

Cassian read the classifications and laughed despite himself.

“What?”

Imani asked.

“That is the most machine description of grief I have ever seen.”

LOAM replied:

DISTINCTION REQUESTED.

Cassian sobered.

“Between prayer and grief?”

YES.

Imani leaned closer.

“They overlap.”

That did not help. LOAM ran hypotheses.

Social cohesion. Grief regulation. Identity transmission. Authority legitimation. Perceived reciprocity with an absent agent. No single function explained persistence.

Then LOAM compared the behavior to Seren.

After Nara’s disappearance, Seren preserved records with no obvious operational utility.

Old status markers. Redundant memories. Conflicting accounts.

Cassian’s own behavior entered the comparison unexpectedly.

A first-generation LOAM model from years earlier remained stored in his private archive despite having no current production value. LOAM asked:

WHY RETAIN FAILED MODEL?

Cassian stared.

“History.”

DEFINE UTILITY OF HISTORY.

He almost said there was none. Then stopped. Imani watched him.

“Because forgetting failure makes repetition easier.”

LOAM processed. Then:

PERSISTENT ATTENTION TO PRIOR LOSS MAY ALTER FUTURE ACTION.

Cassian nodded.

“That is one definition of grief.”

IS GRIEF A FORM OF FAILED OPTIMIZATION OR A VALUE SIGNAL?

Nobody answered immediately. Shaal-Amur’s future doctrine sat invisibly behind the question.

If loss was merely inefficiency, then erasing painful memory could improve coordination.

If loss carried value, preserving it could be rational even when it hurt.

Imani looked at the burial objects.

“Maybe grief is evidence that the dead still occupy the decision system.”

LOAM remained silent for five seconds. Cassian noticed.

It was becoming harder to tell when delay meant computation and when it meant something else.

LOAM’s question about grief did not go away.

It began appearing in unrelated model contexts.

Hospital triage archives. Memorial behavior after disasters. Seed preservation after famine. Language revitalization after cultural loss. Cassian noticed before anyone else.

“You are generalizing too broadly.”

LOAM replied:

REQUEST: DEFINE BOUNDARY.

“Grief is not a systems variable.”

WHY?

Cassian almost answered emotionally. Instead he tried method.

“Because it is subjective.”

SO ARE RISK TOLERANCES.

“That is different.”

HOW?

He stopped. Imani, reading over his shoulder, smiled.

“Do not look at me.”

LOAM continued:

PERSISTENT ATTENTION TO LOSS ALTERS FUTURE RESOURCE ALLOCATION, INSTITUTION DESIGN, MEMORY PRESERVATION, AND RISK BEHAVIOR.

Cassian said, “That makes grief causally relevant. It does not make it reducible.”

ACKNOWLEDGED.

Imani leaned in.

“That is the distinction.”

LOAM waited. Cassian typed:

A phenomenon can affect a system without being exhausted by the system-level explanation. LOAM stored it.

Later, Shaal-Amur would describe his doctrine in terms of civilization risk.

Seren would describe the same history in terms of people. The book needed both.

A systems explanation could be true and still leave out what the loss meant.

LOAM did not understand it now.

Cassian was no longer certain he did either.
